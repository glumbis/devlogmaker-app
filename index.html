Your commits, turned into a narrated devlog video.

DevlogMaker reads what changed in your game since the last devlog, has Claude write the script, lets you edit every line and shot, voices it with ElevenLabs and renders a finished video. It posts to YouTube, Discord, Bluesky or Mastodon only when you confirm.

How it works

From git log to finished video in four steps

You keep making the game. DevlogMaker does the writing, voicing and editing, and stops for your approval where it matters.

  1. Point it at your game

    Pick your game's folder (a git repository). DevlogMaker collects the commits, filtered code changes, docs and new art since your last devlog.

  2. Claude writes the script

    Changes are grouped into topics players care about, then turned into narration and a shot list at the length, tone and audience you choose.

  3. You review and edit

    The run pauses so you can rewrite narration and swap shots. After rendering, reorder and retime clips on the timeline and render again.

  4. Voice, render, publish

    ElevenLabs voices the script, FFmpeg renders the video with captions and music, and it's published to the platforms you pick after you confirm.

Features

Built for how game developers actually work

Reads your real history

Commits, filtered diffs, docs and new assets. Generated files, lockfiles and vendored code are dropped, and you can exclude anything with ignore rules.

A script you control

Choose length, tone (casual, hype, technical, cozy), audience and depth. Edit the narration and shots before anything is voiced.

Natural narration

Pick an ElevenLabs voice, teach it how to say your made-up names, and change one paragraph without re-voicing the rest.

Footage from your game

Use your own clips, record the game window from any engine, capture web games and sites straight from the browser, or add the capture plugin for Godot or Unity (early) for smooth, scripted shots.

Code, diff and title cards

Syntax-highlighted code, "+1,204 / −380 lines" stats and title cards in your brand colors, so every video looks finished even without footage.

Landscape or Shorts

16:9 1080p or 9:16 vertical, with burned-in captions or an .srt file, background music ducked under the voice, and a thumbnail.

Publish after you confirm

YouTube (title, description, tags, chapters, captions, thumbnail; unlisted by default), Discord, Bluesky and Mastodon, plus a ready-to-paste post for itch.io and Steam.

Runs on your computer

Projects, clips and videos stay on your machine. Keys are kept in the system keychain. Only the text the AI needs is sent to it.

Know the cost first

Before each run you see the estimated cost. A typical devlog is well under a dollar on your own keys.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What do I need to use it?

A Windows PC (64-bit; a Mac version is coming later), git installed, and a game project that is a git repository. FFmpeg and everything else is bundled. See system requirements.

Does it work with my engine?

Yes. With no setup, videos use code and diff cards, title cards, new images from your repo and clips you drop into a folder. Window recording works with any engine. For scripted, scene-accurate shots there are capture plugins for Godot and Unity, which are still early.

Do I need to know video editing?

No. DevlogMaker cuts the shots to the narration, adds captions, music and transitions, and renders the video. You can still reorder and retime clips on the timeline if you want to.

What gets sent to the AI? Is my code private?

Your project stays on your computer. To write the script, DevlogMaker sends Claude (Anthropic) your commit messages, the filtered code changes, your project docs, file names and your game's name and pitch. To voice it, the narration text goes to ElevenLabs. Gameplay clips and the rendered video are not sent to either.

You can keep files out with ignore rules. Code cards show code from your repo in the video, so check them during review if your code is private. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

Is it really free?

Yes. DevlogMaker itself costs nothing and needs no account. The AI services cost what their providers charge: ElevenLabs for the narration, and either your Anthropic API key or your existing Claude plan (through Claude Desktop) for the script.

Can I use it without an Anthropic API key?

Yes, with Claude Desktop. In DevlogMaker open Settings, choose Claude Desktop and Connect, then restart Claude Desktop. Now you can ask Claude to set up your game and make a devlog: it inspects your project folder, picks the settings, and writes the script itself. You review it, and DevlogMaker voices and renders the video.

Will it post anything without asking me?

No. Publishing always needs your explicit confirmation, and YouTube uploads are unlisted by default. You can also skip publishing and upload the video yourself.

Who owns the videos and scripts?

You do. DevlogMaker claims no ownership of your projects, scripts or videos. AI-written text can contain mistakes, so review it before you publish. See the License Agreement.

Why does Windows warn me when I open it?

The current builds aren't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen shows a warning on first launch. The download page explains how to open it.
